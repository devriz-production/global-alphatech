Almost every business owner we speak to asks the same question in the first call: should we do SEO or Google Ads? The honest answer is that they do different jobs, and the right starting point depends on how quickly you need enquiries and how long you plan to compete.

## The difference in one paragraph

**SEO** (search engine optimisation) is the work that helps your website appear in Google's free, organic results. It is slow to start and keeps paying off for a long time. **Google Ads** places your business at the top of the results straight away, and you pay each time someone clicks. It is fast, measurable and stops the moment you stop paying.

| | SEO | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first results | Weeks for fixes, months for competitive terms | Days |
| Cost model | Ongoing work, no cost per click | You pay for every click |
| When you stop | Rankings fade slowly | Traffic stops immediately |
| Best for | Long term, compounding growth | Testing demand, quick enquiries |

## Start with Google Ads if...

- You need enquiries this month, for example to fill a new clinic's appointment book or launch a course.
- You are not sure which services or messages customers respond to. Ads tell you within weeks, and those lessons feed your SEO later.
- Your market is seasonal, such as admissions, travel or festive retail, and you need to be visible at specific times.

Keep the first campaign tight: a few high intent keywords, one well built landing page and proper conversion tracking. Without tracking you are guessing, and guessing is expensive.

## Start with SEO if...

- You are in it for the long run and can wait a few months for results.
- Ads in your industry are very expensive per click, which is common in legal, finance, real estate and some healthcare searches.
- You serve a local area. Local SEO and a complete Google Business Profile often bring nearby customers faster than you might expect.

## Why most businesses end up doing both

The two channels make each other better. Ads reveal which keywords actually turn into customers, so SEO can focus on those. Strong organic rankings mean you can spend less on ads for the same visibility. And appearing in both the paid and organic results for the same search builds trust.

A sensible pattern for a small business:

1. **Months 1 to 3:** fix technical SEO and your Google Business Profile, and run a small, tightly tracked ads campaign.
2. **Months 3 to 6:** publish the pages and articles your ads data says matter most; cut ad spend on terms where you now rank well.
3. **Month 6 onwards:** let SEO carry more of the load, and use ads for new services, offers and seasonal pushes.

## What not to do

- Do not run ads to your homepage. Send people to a page built for the exact thing they searched.
- Do not buy SEO packages that promise a number of backlinks or guaranteed rankings.
- Do not judge either channel on clicks alone. Judge them on enquiries and sales.

## The short answer

If you need enquiries now, start with Google Ads and use what you learn. If you can wait and want lasting results, start with SEO. If you can, do both in the right balance.
