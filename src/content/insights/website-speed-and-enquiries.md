You can spend well on ads, write great copy and still lose customers on a slow website. Most of your visitors arrive on a phone, often on a patchy mobile connection. If the page takes too long to appear, or jumps around while it loads, many of them simply go back and tap the next result.

Speed affects your business in three ways:

1. **Fewer people stay.** Every extra second of waiting loses visitors before they see your offer.
2. **Ads cost more.** Google takes landing page experience into account, so a slow page can push up what you pay for each click.
3. **Rankings suffer.** Page experience is one of the signals Google uses, and a fast site is easier for Google to crawl.

## What Google measures: Core Web Vitals

Google sums up page experience with three measurements, called Core Web Vitals:

| Measure | What it means | Good score |
|---|---|---|
| Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) | How long the main content takes to appear | 2.5 seconds or less |
| Interaction to Next Paint (INP) | How quickly the page responds when someone taps or clicks | 200 milliseconds or less |
| Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) | How much the page jumps around while loading | 0.1 or less |

## Test your site in five minutes

1. Open **PageSpeed Insights** and enter your homepage address.
2. Look at the **mobile** results first. That is what most of your visitors experience.
3. If you see "Core Web Vitals Assessment: Failed", note which of the three measures is in red.
4. Repeat for your most important pages: the ones your ads and Google listing send people to.
5. In **Google Search Console**, the Core Web Vitals report shows which groups of pages need work across your whole site.

## The fixes that make the biggest difference

**Images.** The most common problem by far. Resize images to the size they are shown at, use modern formats such as WebP or AVIF, and load images further down the page only when they are needed.

**Hosting.** Cheap shared hosting is often the bottleneck. Good hosting with caching, and a content delivery network for visitors across India, can cut load times substantially.

**Plugins and scripts.** Every chat widget, slider, tracking tag and plugin adds weight. Remove what you do not use, and load what remains after the main content.

**Fonts.** Use one or two font families, host them yourself and load only the weights you need.

**Layout shifts.** Give images, videos and ad slots fixed dimensions so the page does not jump while they load.

**Themes and page builders.** Heavy multipurpose themes load code for features you will never use. Sometimes a lean rebuild is cheaper than endless patching.

## When to fix and when to rebuild

If your site fails on one or two measures, targeted fixes are usually enough. If it fails on all three, runs on an outdated theme and is hard for your team to update, a rebuild on a lighter platform often pays for itself through better conversion and lower ad costs.
